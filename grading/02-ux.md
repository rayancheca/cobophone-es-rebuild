## 1. Primary CTA clarity — 7/10

**Evidence:** `HomeHero.tsx` lines 50-64 render two equally-weighted XL buttons side-by-side: `ctaPrimary` ("Calcular precio") in `variant="primary"` and `ctaSecondary` ("Hablar por WhatsApp") in `variant="whatsapp"`. Both are `size="xl"`. The WhatsApp button uses the recognizable `#25D366` green, which actually has *higher* visual weight than the brand blue. The hero also displays an `eyebrow`, headline, sub, quote ("Si no sabemos el fallo, es que no existe."), 4-cell trust strip, and 3 inline badges — a lot of competing focus targets.

**Hick's Law:** Two CTAs is fine; the green outshouting the blue is not. WhatsApp is the *escape hatch*, not the primary funnel. Visual hierarchy contradicts business priority (quote → booking is measurable; WhatsApp is a free-form conversation that's hard to attribute).

**Fix:** Make Calcular precio visually dominant (solid blue, larger, with a subtle arrow nudge animation). Demote WhatsApp to ghost/outline. Keep both — Spanish repair customers expect WhatsApp — but make the visual order match funnel order.

**File:** `src/components/sections/HomeHero.tsx:49-64`, `src/components/ui/Button.tsx` (variant weights).

## 2. Conversion funnel logic — 8/10

**Evidence:** Home `/` → `/presupuesto` → 6 ordered steps → confirmation. The URL is deep-linkable at every step (`?dispositivo=movil&marca=apple&modelo=iphone-15-pro&reparacion=pantalla` jumps straight to step 5). Per-model SEO pages (`/reparacion/movil/apple/iphone-15-pro`) link into the same quote flow with state pre-filled. This is the architecture Back Market and Phone House aspire to and rarely execute.

**Weakness:** Step 6 "confirmation" is a **simulated** confirmation — `markSubmitted()` in `QuoteTool.tsx:126` flips local state, no booking actually happens. Copy says "Resumen" with model + repair, but there's no calendar slot, no address, no SMS confirmation, no ticket number. A stressed customer at this point will WhatsApp to verify, which leaks the lead to an unmeasured channel.

**Fix:** Either (a) make step 5's "Reservar reparación" CTA open a real calendar/slot picker, or (b) be honest in copy: "Te llamamos en 5 minutos para confirmar hora" with a real promise.

**File:** `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx:422-452` (Step6Confirmation).

## 3. Friction points — 7/10

**Evidence:**
- No password walls anywhere — checked all 7 pages
- No modal interrupters
- Contact form has 4 fields + 1 checkbox (`/contacto`), which is at Baymard's recommended minimum
- Quote tool input types are correct: `type="search"` on the model search field (`QuoteTool.tsx:242`), proper `tel` and `email` on the contact form
- Step 5 "Reservar reparación" doesn't ask for anything — clicks straight through to confirmation

**Friction sources:**
- B2B `/mayoristas` form has **6+ fields** (company, CIF, phone, email, volume dropdown, multi-checkbox parts, optional message) — appropriate for B2B intent, fine.
- The "Acepto la política de privacidad" checkbox on `/contacto` is mandatory and the copy is full-legalese ("y el tratamiento de mis datos"). Baymard finding: required consent checkboxes drop submission ~7-12% when phrased legalistically. Soften to "Acepto que CoboPhone me contacte sobre esta reparación".
- No address required at any point in the consumer quote tool, which is excellent.

**File:** `src/components/sections/ContactForm.tsx`, `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx`.

## 4. Trust signals at decision moments — 6/10

**Cialdini lens:**

| Principle | Top of home | Price reveal (Step 5) | Confirmation (Step 6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social proof | 6 Google reviews, "20.000+ reparaciones" | none | none |
| Authority | "20 años en Cobo Calleja" | "Diagnóstico gratuito" chip | none |
| Reciprocity | "Sin compromiso" | "Sin compromiso" implied | none |
| Commitment | none | "Rango cerrado. Lo confirmamos en 2 minutos" | weak ("Resumen") |
| Scarcity | none | none | none |
| Liking | "Pasa, te recibimos →" | none | none |
| Unity (Madrid) | "Cobo Calleja", "Madrid desde 2005" | none | none |

**The price-reveal moment is undertrussted.** This is the screen where the customer decides whether to spend €299 or buy a new phone — and the trust pills (`Clock 40 min`, `ShieldCheck 3m garantía`, `Check Diagnóstico gratuito`) are small chips at `text-sm`. There are no reviews, no "1,247 iPhone 15 Pro screens repaired this year", no technician photo, no warranty terms link.

**Strength:** The anchoring panel (`QuoteTool.tsx:385-393`) — *"Comprar un iPhone 15 Pro nuevo cuesta unos 1219 €. Repararlo cuesta unos 299–379 €"* — is excellent. This is the Cialdini play of the audit. But it's surrounded by white space; it needs a green checkmark or accent to make the anchor land harder.

**Fix:** Inject one short Google review specific to the model/repair at step 5. Add "Hoy reparamos N pantallas de iPhone" counter (real or programmatic).

**File:** `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx:340-420`.

## 5. Mobile-first — 8/10

**Evidence:**
- Hero CTAs stack vertical on mobile (`flex flex-col sm:flex-row`), full-width tap targets at `h-14` (56px — exceeds Apple HIG 44px and Google 48dp)
- Quote tool device grid is `grid-cols-2 md:grid-cols-3 lg:grid-cols-4` — phones get 2-up cards, not a cramped 7-up row
- Inputs use `inputMode`/`type` correctly (`tel`, `email`, `search`)
- WhatsApp links use `wa.me` (deep-links to the installed app on mobile)
- The hero device placeholder is `hidden lg:block` — wisely not shipped on mobile
- Price reveal uses fluid typography: `clamp(3rem, 2rem + 6vw, 6rem)` (`QuoteTool.tsx:360`) — €299 renders huge on mobile without overflow

**Weakness:**
- The 4-cell trust strip below the hero (`grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-4`) stacks 2×2 on mobile, pushing CTAs further below the fold than they need to be
- Step 4 repair-type list uses `flex items-center gap-4` with name + description + duration + warranty in a single row — risks text wrap on 320px iPhone SE
- No `viewport-fit=cover` for safe-area on notched phones (not visible in fetched content)

**File:** `src/components/sections/HomeHero.tsx:80`, `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx:301-336`.

## 6. Speed-to-price — 9/10

**Cold path (someone landing on `/`):**
1. Tap "Calcular precio" (1 tap)
2. Tap "Móvil" (1 tap)
3. Tap "Apple" (1 tap)
4. Tap "iPhone 13" (1 tap)
5. Tap "Pantalla" (1 tap)

= **5 taps, ~25 seconds**, price visible.

**Warm path (deep link from Google search):**
`/reparacion/movil/apple/iphone-15-pro` shows a full price table immediately. Or the deep-link query string lands on Step 5 with zero interaction.

**Vs competition:**
- Back Market: ~8 taps, but it's a marketplace
- Phone House: usually requires a form submission to even get a range
- uBreakiFix: 3 taps, but US-only

CoboPhone is **best-in-Madrid** on this axis. The only nit is that the cold path has 7 device categories at step 1 (Hick) when 99% of traffic is `movil` — see #8.

**File:** `src/app/reparacion/[device]/[brand]/[model]/page.tsx`, `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx:36-45` (URL hydration).

## 7. Error & empty states — 8/10

**404 (`not-found.tsx`):**

```
Error 404
Esta página no existe.
Pero tu reparación sí. Calcula el precio en 30 segundos
o escríbenos por WhatsApp.
[Calcular precio →] [WhatsApp]
¿Te perdiste? Volver al inicio
```

This is genuinely good. Recovery-oriented, on-brand voice, two CTAs that align with the funnel, and a fallback home link. Lines 5-38.

**Quote tool no-match (`Step3`):** When a model search returns 0 results, copy reads "No encontramos tu modelo" with a WhatsApp escape pre-filled with the brand name (`QuoteTool.tsx:276-289`). This is excellent — the empty state actively converts.

**Deep-link to invalid model:** `/reparacion/movil/apple/iphone-zzz-nonexistent` returns HTTP 404 (verified). Good, this means the route's `generateStaticParams` is gating correctly rather than rendering a broken price table.

**Gap:** What happens if `getPrice(modelSlug, repairSlug)` returns null at step 5? Code at `QuoteTool.tsx:365-367` handles it: "Consulta el precio por WhatsApp". But this is the *peak* moment — falling back to WhatsApp here forfeits the conversion to an unmeasurable channel. Consider always showing *some* range with a tighter band rather than punting.

**File:** `src/app/not-found.tsx`, `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx:276-289, 365-367`.

## 8. Cognitive load — 6/10

**Step 1 — 7 device categories** (Móvil, Tablet, Portátil, Smartwatch, Consola, Televisor, Patinete). This is the **upper bound of Hick's 7±1**, and the visual treatment is uniform — same icon size, same card, same description weight. Result: a customer with a cracked iPhone has to scan seven equal options to find the obvious one.

**Fix:** Promote "Móvil" visually — make it a 2×span card with "El 92% de nuestras reparaciones" copy, then a smaller grid for the other six. Same number of choices, lower scan cost.

**Step 2 — Brands.** Code limits visible brands to 7 with a "Ver todos (+N)" disclosure (`QuoteTool.tsx:188, 208-217`). Good Hick application.

**Step 3 — Models.** Search input + grid sorted by `popularityScore` descending, "Popular" badge on the first card (`QuoteTool.tsx:256-260`). Excellent — this is Goal-Gradient + Social Proof + Decoy in one component.

**Step 4 — Repair types.** Variable list per model. "Popular" badge on first item. Fine.

**Step 5 — Price.** Single big number + 3 chips + anchoring + 2 CTAs. Slightly busy but acceptable.

**Step 6 — Confirmation.** Sparse, almost too sparse — see #9.

**Goal-gradient verdict:** Progress bar weights are explicit at `QuoteTool.tsx:58`: `{1: 0, 2: 0.30, 3: 0.60, 4: 0.85, 5: 1}`. This **accelerates** correctly — step 4→5 is the smallest visual gap (15%), which gives the "almost there" dopamine. Strong implementation. 0.85→1.0 could be even tighter (e.g., 0.92) for an even harder pull at the end.

## 9. Peak-end moments — 6/10

**Peak (Step 5 price reveal):**

The number is correctly the hero of the screen — `clamp(3rem, 2rem + 6vw, 6rem)`, font-mono, tabular-nums, tracking-tight. The "€299–€379" anchor against "€1219 new" is a solid Cialdini punch. **But:**

- No animated count-up on the price (a 600ms ease-out count from 0 → 299 would lock in the peak)
- No "Reservar reparación" microcopy below the button ("Bloqueamos tu hueco mañana 11:30")
- The 3 trust chips look like form pills, not laurels — they should feel like medals
- No customer photo, no technician photo, nothing human

**End (Step 6 confirmation):**

```
[green checkmark]
"Recibido. Te llamamos en 5 minutos."
Resumen:
iPhone 15 Pro
Pantalla · 3 meses de garantía
```

This is **anticlimactic** for a peak-end model. Per Kahneman, the "end" is half the memory. A stressed customer who just decided to spend €299 needs:
- A specific time commitment ("Te llamamos antes de las 18:30")
- A reference number
- A photo or name of the technician
- A "qué traer" checklist (ID, find-my-iPhone off, etc.)
- A calendar download (.ics)
- Tracking link

Right now they get a green check and a "Resumen" card.

**Fix:** Treat Step 6 like a delivery receipt, not a form-submit thank-you.

**File:** `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx:424-452`.

## 10. Accessibility implied by UX — 8/10

**Evidence:**
- Hero uses semantic `<section aria-labelledby="hero-heading">` with matched `<h1 id="hero-heading">` (`HomeHero.tsx:23, 37-43`)
- Each quote step has `<section aria-labelledby="stepN-h">` + matching `<h2 id="stepN-h">` (`QuoteTool.tsx:158-159, 190, 239, etc.`)
- Decorative icons use `aria-hidden` consistently (`ArrowRight`, `MessageCircle`, etc.)
- Buttons have visible text labels (no icon-only buttons in primary flow)
- 404 has `<h1>` + recovery links
- Quote tool back buttons are real `<button type="button">` with text, not icon-only
- The chatbot/WhatsApp escape hatch is keyboard-reachable

**Gaps:**
- The hero device placeholder has 12 floating app icons with animated `transform: translateY`. No `prefers-reduced-motion` guard visible in `HomeHero.tsx:182-211` keyframes. CLAUDE.md says reduced-motion must be honored everywhere — this slot isn't.
- Progress bar at `QuoteTool.tsx:82-88` has `aria-hidden` but no `<progress>` element or `role="progressbar"` with `aria-valuenow` — screen readers won't announce step progress
- Focus styles on the device cards rely on `hover:ring-brand-primary` — need explicit `focus-visible:ring-2`
- The chatbot bottom-left (per brief) wasn't visible in WebFetch — verify it has a proper `aria-label`, focus trap, and ESC-to-close

**File:** `src/components/sections/HomeHero.tsx:182-211`, `src/components/quote/QuoteTool.tsx:82-88`.

## Verdict

Brutally: **CoboPhone's prototype is better engineered than Phone House's live site, but it cosplay-converts where Back Market actually-converts.** The funnel architecture (deep-linkable URL state, programmatic per-model SEO pages, goal-gradient progress weights at 0/30/60/85/100, anchoring against MSRP, WhatsApp escape hatches without blocking the main flow) is in the top decile of the category — most Spanish repair shops still hide prices behind a contact form. But the **end** of the funnel — Step 6 — is a façade: there's no real reservation, no calendar slot, no SMS, no ticket. A category leader like uBreakiFix locks in a slot and sends a confirmation email at this moment; CoboPhone says "Recibido" and hopes. The peak-end model says people remember the peak (the €299 reveal — currently mid-strength) and the end (currently weak). Fix Step 6, add one review at the price reveal, and demote WhatsApp green in the hero, and this site moves from "well-built Madrid repair shop" to "the one everyone copies in 2026."
